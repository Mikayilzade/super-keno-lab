The article states that the private league is structured so that participants can compete for a prize each round, including people joining after the season starts.

Classification:

`current_repeatable_zero_cost_cash_competition_adjacent_not_super_keno_modifier`

Why it matters:

1. It proves that APL private-league denominators can be in the **hundreds**, even though the global platform is >10,000 users.
2. It gives a concrete scale benchmark for the unresolved Misli private league; the Misli denominator cannot be inferred, but it is no longer reasonable to assume automatically that all global users participate.
3. The prize is cash, so there is no bonus-wallet/product-scope ambiguity for this separate competition.
4. It is not counted as a Super Keno modifier because winning the cash is a separate skill-competition outcome rather than a subsidy attached to a Super Keno ticket.

If one used a deliberately naive equal-chance model purely as a scale diagnostic, 50 AZN / 500 participants is about **0.10 AZN face-value competition EV per entrant per round**. This is not a valid player-specific EV because fantasy skill is heterogeneous and the reported denominator is `500+`, not exactly 500.

## 5. Strategic consequence

The APL Fantasy route splits into two distinct branches:

- **Misli private league:** stronger prize frequency/face value, but bonus product scope and denominator unresolved;
- **cash private leagues:** immediately cash-valued and denominator sometimes published, but not directly a Super Keno modifier.

For the Super Keno project, Misli remains the lead only if the bonus can be proven Lotereya-eligible. Otherwise the cash-league discovery is retained as evidence that zero-cost external competitions can produce liquid capital, not as proof of positive Super Keno EV.
